What happens when you ask

Public companies file long reports with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission every quarter and every year. This site reads about 370 of them so you don't have to. Here's what it does with your question, in four steps.

  1. 1

    Work out which company and year you mean

    Asking about 3M in 2022 narrows hundreds of reports down to the few from 3M around 2022.

  2. 2

    Look up the numbers, or find the right pages

    For figures like debt or cash flow, it uses the exact numbers companies report to the SEC and does the math itself. For everything else, it searches the reports for the passages most likely to hold the answer.

  3. 3

    Write an answer from only what it found

    An AI model, Claude, reads those passages and numbers and writes a short answer, marking each source with a number like [2].

  4. 4

    Let you check every claim

    Each number links to the original filing on the SEC website, with the page and section.

How often is it right?

On FinanceBench, a public test of 150 questions written by financial analysts, it answers about 87 in 100 correctly. Think of it as a fast research assistant: useful, not infallible. Check the cited page before relying on an answer.

What it won't do

It only knows the 32 companies below, from 2015 to 2023. It doesn't give investment advice or look at today's stock price. If it can't tell which company you mean, it says so instead of guessing.
